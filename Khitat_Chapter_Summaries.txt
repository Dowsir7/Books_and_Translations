AL-MAQRIZI, "DESCRIPTION TOPOGRAPHIQUE ET HISTORIQUE DE L'EGYPTE"
(Bouriant translation, Parts 1-2) -- CHAPTER SUMMARIES
================================================================================
Companion to the full English translation (bouriant_complete_en_normalized_2026-06-09.txt).
Page numbers refer to the original Bouriant edition. Each part numbers its
chapters independently (I-XLVI in the First Part, I-LXXXV in the Second).

NOTE ON COVERAGE: The Second-Part summaries below are detailed, drawn from
per-chapter notes made during translation. The First-Part summaries are briefer,
reconstructed from chapter titles and general content; consult the full text for

================================================================================
FIRST PART
================================================================================

I. A glance at the figure of the heavens -- Cosmological preliminaries: the
   structure of the sky, celestial spheres, and the framing of Egypt within the
   created order.

II. Of the form of the earth and the distribution of its climes -- The earth's
   shape and the system of the seven climes (climatic zones).

III. Of the situation of Egypt and its place among the seven climes -- Where
   Egypt falls within the clime scheme.

IV. Limits and orientation of Egypt -- The boundaries and cardinal extent of the
   country.

V. Of the sea of Qolzoum -- The Red Sea (Qolzoum/Suez side).

VI. Of the sea of Roum -- The Mediterranean.

VII. Of the name Masr, its meaning, and the different names of Egypt -- Etymology
   and the various names borne by Egypt.

VIII. A glance at Egypt's relations with religion -- Egypt's standing in sacred
   history and scripture.

IX. Of the particularities one meets in Egypt -- Notable natural and cultural
   features peculiar to the country.

X. Of the marvels of Egypt: talismans, temples, and the like -- The wonder-works
   of the ancients.

XI. Of the hiding-places and treasures (montaleb) -- Buried hoards and the
   treasure-seeking tradition.

XII. Of the destruction of the riches of Egypt -- How the country's wealth was
   dispersed and ruined over time.

XIII. Of the character, manners, and temperament of the Egyptians.

XIV. A few words on the particularities of the Nile.

XV. Of the source of the Nile and its course.

XVI. Refutation of those who deny the Nile's flood comes from a torrent.

XVII. Of the measuring of the Nile and its flood -- Nilometry.

XVIII. Of the bridge by which one crossed the Nile.

XIX. Of what has been said of the Nile's water, in praise or blame.

XX. Of the marvels of the Nile.

XXI. On the means of foreseeing the Nile's state each year.

XXII. Of the feast of the Martyrs.

XXIII. Of the canals derived from the Nile.

XXIV. Of the state of Egypt in the first times.

XXV. Of the provinces of Egypt and its nomes.

XXVI. How canals were dug and dikes raised to retain and distribute the Nile.

XXVII. Of the sum of Egypt's revenues in ancient times.

XXVIII. How the Muslims organized taxes at the conquest, and the situation of
   Egypt and the Copts.

XXIX. Of the uprisings of the Copts and their consequences.

XXX. Of the arrival of the Arabs in the Egyptian countryside, their seizure of
   the cultivations, and attendant events.

XXXI. Of the farming of Egypt's lands after the spread of Islam and the Arab
   settlement, up to El-Naser's second cadastre.

XXXII. Of the last cadastre of El-Naser.

XXXIII. Of the divan (the administrative bureau).

XXXIV. Of the divan of the troops and the army.

XXXV. Of the parceling of land and the fiefs.

XXXVI. Of the divan of revenue and tax.

XXXVII. Of the revenues of Egypt under Islam.

XXXVIII. Of the diverse lands of Egypt and the distribution of cultivations --
   includes the agricultural calendar (when each crop is sown, transplanted, and
   ripens by Coptic month).

XXXIX. Classification of the revenues of Egypt.

XL. Of the pyramids -- their builders, dimensions, legends, and the accounts of
   their construction (Sourid and the pre-Flood warning).

XLI. Of the statue named "the Terrible" -- the Sphinx.

XLII. Of the mountains.

XLIII. Of Mount Moqattam.

XLIV. Of the Red mountain.

XLV. Of the mountain of Yachkour.

XLVI. Of the Observatory (el-Rased). -- End of the First Part.

================================================================================
SECOND PART
================================================================================

I. Of the towns of the land of Egypt -- Introductory survey of Egypt's cities.

II. Of the town of Amsous, its marvels and kings -- Amsous and the antediluvian
   kings.

III. Of the town of Memphis and its kings -- Memphis and its long king-lists,
   running down to Nebuchadnezzar.

IV. Of the town of Alexandria -- Foundation lore: Raqoudah, the Gouriaq/Giroun
   legend, Dhu'l Qorneïn, Sheddad's musnad, Alexander's founding, and the
   lighthouses-as-talismans.

V. Of Alexander.

VI. Of the era of Alexander -- The Seleucid calendar.

VII. On the difference between Alexander and Dhu'l Qorneïn (two distinct figures).

VIII. Of the kings who reigned at Alexandria after Alexander -- The Ptolemies
   (Philadelphus and the Septuagint, Cleopatra), the Persian decade, Heraclius.

IX. Of the Pharos of Alexandria -- The glass-crab pedestal, the sun-tracking
   statues, the earthquakes, and El-Oualid's mirror ruse.

X. Of the circus at Alexandria and other marvels -- Plus 'Amr's first visit.

XI. Of Pompey's Pillar ("the column of the masts") -- With a digression on giant
   stature.

XII. A glance at what has been said of Alexandria.

XIII. Of the conquest of Alexandria -- Years 20-21, El Maqoqos, the baths-duel,
   'Abadat.

XIV. Of what the Muslims did at Alexandria and the Greek uprisings -- Manuel, the
   razing of the walls, the Dhu'l Souari naval battle (year 34).

XV. Of the lake of Alexandria.

XVI. Of the canal of Alexandria -- The El-Makhzoumi register; the dredging history
   down to Barsebaï (826).

XVII. Collection of traditions on Alexandria -- The Andalusian episode (199-212),
   the Fatimid pushes, the Ayyubid/Crusader era (El-Babein 562, with the young
   Saladin), and the 727 riot (the Frankish-merchant assault, the gate-crush, 30
   rebels sawn apart, the vizier's punitive aftermath).

XVIII. Of the town of Athrib -- Ibn Ouacif Shah's marvel-city.

XIX. Of the town of Tennis -- Named for Tennis son of Cham; Qlimoun; the "two
   gardens"; the founding of Damietta; geography and lake-formation; the conquest
   by Abou Thour; the "el badnah" weaving and Ka'bah-veils; the long El-Amin /
   El-Mamoun civil war and the rebel El-Geraoui (the rope-trick capture of
   El-Seri); marvels (the giant fish, the nine fiery columns, the two-headed
   girl); Frankish/Sicilian raids and El-Kamel's destruction (624).

XX. Of the town of Sa -- Marhoun's mirror-towers, giant-grained wheat, the
   water-to-wine statuette.

XXI. Of the sand of the Occident -- Sheddad ben Haddad's destroyed Adite works as
   the origin of the sand; Maraqiah, Kom Sherik, Samanoud.

XXII. Of the town of Balbaïs -- Biblical Goshen; Armanosah taken and returned by
   'Amr; Amaury's siege.

XXIII. Of the country of El Ouaradah -- Ibn Khordadabeh's mileages; founded by
   El-Saleh Negm el din (644).

XXIV. Of the town of Eilah -- The sabbath-breakers turned to apes and swine; the
   Prophet's safe-conduct (year 9); the natron-valley of Ouadi Habib and its
   70,000 monks.

XXV. Of the town of Madian -- Sho'aïb's people; the six alphabet-kings
   (Abgad/Hawaz/...); the burning-wind destruction; the El A'oug well of musnad
   books on Moses.

XXVI. End of the accounts of Madian -- Abgad's five sons; Malek ben Da'r's 24
   sons, ruling 500 years until Solomon.

XXVII. Of the town of Faran -- The Amalecite hilltop town; Pharaoh's
   drowning-shore.

XXVIII. Of the land of Gofar -- Five towns; the etymology from foundered camels;
   Khodam's buried land.

XXIX. Of the Said of Egypt -- "the High Land"; Misraïm's division; El-Edfaoui's
   geography; the scorpion-sorceress; the decline from the "year of anguish" (776)
   through the failed flood of 806.

XXX. Of the cataracts and Nubia -- El-Assouani's survey: the Nubian march, the
   rock-rapids, the "Lord of the Mountain," slave-barter, Dongola, the mines, the
   Souakin road.

XXXI. Of the Nile from 'Alouah and the riverside peoples -- Nubians and Maqorrah;
   Moses destroying the star-worshippers; the White and Green Niles meeting at
   Soubah; El Kanem and Mali (the hidden king, "dendi" cloth-money, the wild
   Fioul, boat-sized gourds); the coming of Islam (school of Ibn Rashiq, 640).

XXXII. Of the Bedjahs -- The Hazabah emerald mines; nomadic matrilineal life; the
   sebaïah lances; the treaties (Ibn El Hagab's, 'Abd Allah ben El Gahm's with
   chief Kenoun, year 216); pagan interior Bedjahs; Masʿudi's account.

XXXIII. Of the town of Assouan -- Etymology ("to grieve"); the frontier post; the
   emerald-mountain and gold mine; the 344-345 Nubian war; the Benou-l Kenz of
   Rabi'ah; the catastrophe and razing after 806.

XXXIV. Of Bilaq (Philae) -- The chief Muslim citadel-island by the cataracts.

XXXV. Of the wall of the Old Woman -- Daloukah's Egypt-encircling rampart with
   bell-relay garrisons, built in six months after Pharaoh drowned.

XXXVI. Of the tribute (baqt) -- The word's meaning; the full convention with King
   Qalidourouth (360 slaves a year); the jurists' dispute; Masʿudi's
   recipient-breakdown; the 694 war (Beibars' expedition, Skandah crowned with new
   tribute); Nubia split, Christians under a dinar capitation.

XXXVII. Of the desert of 'Aïdhab -- The pilgrim/merchant route Qous-'Aïdhab-
   Djeddah; the reed-hut port eclipsed by 'Aden then Djeddah; the pearl-fisheries;
   the nail-less coconut-sewn boats; the naked lawless Bedjahs; the Simoum heat.

XXXVIII. Of the town of Louqsor (Luxor) -- An important Said town inhabited by the
   Marisi, source of the "marisiah" asses.

XXXIX. Of Baliana -- El-Edfaoui's anecdote of the dismissed vali of Qous and the
   imam: the sixty milk-dishes vs. "we also give cakes"; the praise-verses for a
   judge; the sugar-presses and the inhabitants' generosity.

XL. Of Samhoud -- A west-bank town with seventeen sugarcane mills, where (it is
   claimed) the rats won't touch the local canes.

XLI. Of Argenous -- The small "well of Siros" outside a church, with its
   25-Bashans feast; the Christians reading the year's Nile flood from its rise.

XLII. Of Abou Bat -- The minaret built to sway right and left when climbed, the
   oscillation visible from the moving shadow on the ground.

XLIII. Of Mellawi -- Sugarcane country and its mills; the children of Fadil
   cultivating 1,500 feddans yearly under El-Naser; the 738 Treasury seizure of
   candy sugar.

XLIV. Of the town of Ansena -- Daloukah's Persian-bonnet nilometer-circus ringed
   by 365 granite columns; the Prophet's concubine and Pharaoh's magicians from
   its village Hafan; the crocodile-repelling talisman; the nosebleed-causing
   bang-wood; Saladin shipping its rampart-stones to Cairo.

XLV. Of El Qeïs -- Named for 'Amr's commander Qeïs ben El Hareth; the wool and
   goat-hair garments unique to Egypt (the aged Moawiah warmed by one); the famed
   Qeïs/Behnesa carpets and tents; the unfathomable underground lake near Behnesa
   (200 divers, the six-day cord-guided boat) under El-Kamel.

XLVI. Of Darout Bilhassah -- The three Darouts; the mosque of the generous Ziad
   ben El Mogheïrah, with elegiac verses for Ziad, his brother Ibrahim, and his
   son Ahmed.

XLVII. Of Sakar -- The stone monument: a giant east-facing camel with an
   inscription, a second facing it, the stone "bales of cloth," and a third facing
   the mountain.

XLVIII. Of Miniet el Khasib -- Named for Khasib ben 'Abd el Hamid, Haroun
   el-Rashid's revenue-intendant.

XLIX. Of Miniet el Nasek -- Named for the cruel Christian vali El Nasek, killed
   and dragged through Qous with a dead dog tied to his foot (531).

L. Of Gizeh -- Etymology; the great Sunday market; "Gizeh is one of the gardens
   of Paradise"; the mosque of Toubah holding Moses's ark-box; Mary's
   fruit-bearing palm; 'Omar's rebuke to 'Amr; the citadel (21-22); the tribal
   land-division; the mosque built under 'Ali ben El Ikhshid with stolen church
   columns; Ka'b el Ahbar's tomb; the crocodile-repelling marbles; El-Naser
   reserving the revenues (724).

LI. Of the prison of Joseph -- At Bousir: the traces of Joseph and Moses;
   El-Sharfi carrying the sheikh up the sand hill for the Gabriel-hadith; the 415
   drum-and-trumpet festival and the populace's carnival.

LII. Of the village of Tersa -- Built by El-Qasem ben Obeïd Allah ben El Hagab;
   the combat against Haroun ben Mohammed El Ga'edi.

LIII. Miniet Andounah -- Named for the Christian secretary Andounah, arrested by
   Ibn Touloun and fined 50,000 dinars.

LIV. Of Ousim -- The emir 'Abd allah ben Merwan dismissed mid-visit to a Copt's
   domain; the host insisting he stay and keep nothing back.

LV. Of Miniet Aqabah -- Named for the Companion 'Aqabah ben 'Amer; his six
   obligations toward the people; his Koran-reading and poetry; his death (58) and
   Moqattam burial.

LVI. Of Helouan -- Named for king Halouan; the life of 'Abd el 'Aziz ben Merwan
   (the plague-flight, the ill-omened messenger, the founding of Helouan, Merwan's
   counsel, the succession quarrel with 'Abd el Melek, his death in 86); plus a
   digression on the floating-vessel principle and the magnetized fish-compass of
   the Indian Ocean.

LVII. Of the town of El Arish -- A post-Flood town; Misraïm's "Gate of Paradise"
   Darsân; Masr ben Beïsar's dream and the thirty at Memphis "Mafat"; Joseph
   meeting his family (El 'Arsh, the throne / the arbor); the Frankish palm-cutting
   (577); Ka'b el Ahbar's ten prophets' tombs.

LVIII. Of the town of Farma -- Named for Alexander's brother (or El Farma ben
   Filiqous); Galen's tomb; El-Moutawakel's citadel (239); 'Amr's peace via
   Abrahah; the Greek raids; the always-fruiting palm; Baldwin's 507 raid and
   death; Tughtakin honored at Ascalon; the 545 Frankish sack and Shaour's
   destruction; the "barrier between two seas."

LIX. Of the town of Qolzoum -- The sea-naming town (later Suez, facing 'Agroud);
   waterless; the Syria-Egypt port; Ibn El Tawir on the ruins and budget; El-Hakem
   remitting ship-duties (387); Ibn Khordadabah's merchant route to Sind, Hind,
   and China.

LX. El Tih -- The 40-year wandering-desert near Aïlah where Moses died; the
   sand-buried "Green Town" found by fleeing Bahri Mamelukes (652), with its
   gazelle-stamped Moses-era dinars and ice-cold cistern, visible only to the lost.

LXI. Of the town of Damietta -- THE LONGEST CHAPTER. Name and etymology; the
   Muslim conquest (the governor El Hamouk, the convert-martyr Sheta); the Greek
   raids (years 90-357) and the 260-cubit monster fish (408); the Crusader/Sicilian
   assaults; the 565 armada repelled by Saladin in 55 days; the fortifications
   (577-588). THE FIFTH CRUSADE (615): the Frankish landing, the chain-castle, the
   concealed death of El-'Adel, El-Kamel's rise, the God-sent wind delivering the
   iron-clad ship, Ibn el-Mashtoub's conspiracy, the fall of Damietta (24 Sha'ban,
   16 months 22 days), the founding of Mansourah, the trench-flood trapping the
   Franks, the surrender and hostage-exchange, Damietta handed back (618). THE
   SEVENTH CRUSADE (647-648): the ailing El-Saleh, Louis IX's arrogant letter and
   the cadi's defiant reply, Fakhr el din's cowardly flight, Damietta abandoned,
   El-Saleh hanging 50+ emirs and dying at Mansourah, the watermelon-headed swimmer,
   the traitor's fords, Fakhr el din killed, Beibars and the Baharite Mamelukes
   routing the Franks through Mansourah's streets, Touran Shah enthroned, the
   decisive victory above Faraskour, Louis IX captured and chained in Ibn Loqman's
   house, the prisoner massacres, Touran Shah's tyranny and murder, Shagarat el-dorr
   made queen, Damietta ceded and Louis released, Ibn Matrouh's and El-Zayat's
   satirical verses (the latter prophesying Louis's death at Tunis), the Bahrite
   razing (648), Beibars blocking the river-mouth with round stones (659), and the
   rebuilt garden-girt present-day Damietta with Maqrizi's praise-verses and the
   biography of the ascetic saint Fateh el Takrouri of the Menged Fatah mosque.

LXII. Of Shata -- The town that names the Shatawiah stuffs; Shata ben El Hamouk,
   convert and martyr at Tennis (year 21); the Ka'bah-veils woven there. Plus the
   localities Barzakh (the swaying brick minaret), Dabiq (the gold-threaded
   turbans), El Nahririah (Sonqor's waqf village), and the island of the Béni
   Naser.

LXIII. Of the route from Masr to Damascus -- The origin of post-relays (Darius's
   docked-tail mounts, "barid"); Ibn Khordadabah's old itinerary; the Frankish
   disruption; El-Saleh founding El-Salahieh (644); Beibars perfecting the relay
   (Cairo-Damascus news in four days), ruined by Tamerlane's sack of Damascus (803).

LXIV. Of the town of Hatein -- Named for the bellicose king Hatein, ruling Egypt
   from a Jordan-mountain citadel; the namesake near Safad with Sho'aib's tomb.

LXV. Of the town of El Raqah -- A Madian locality inhabited at the Exodus by an
   ox-worshipping Lakhm tribe related to Pharaoh (the reason the Samaritan made the
   calf).

LXVI. Of Aïn Shams (Heliopolis) -- The old town Ra'msâs and the Sabean
   star-worship: the twelve geometric temples, the planet-angel theology, the seven
   planet-temples (the Ka'bah, Tyre, Damascus, Aïn Shams, Mantih, Saïda, Harran);
   king Menqâous and the buried wife-statue; Pythagoras tested by the priests; the
   obelisks "of the Pharaoh" (the green-oxidized copper caps, the 656 fall with
   200 qantars of copper); the solstice observation; Ibn Touloun and the "speaking"
   statue; and the balsam of Matarieh (the Holy Family's flight, Mary washing the
   garments, the venerated well, the Christian "miroun").

LXVII. Of Mansourah -- Founded by El-Kamel in 616 facing the Franks at Damietta;
   the victory-celebration after the recovery (the slave-girls' duelling songs, the
   cadi Habbah Allah's qasidah pointing to El-Mo'azem/El-Ashraf/El-Kamel).

LXVIII. El Abbassah -- The royal pleasure-spot between Balbeïs and Salahieh,
   favored by El-Kamel (the hunting, the still-hot bread from the Citadel); named
   for Abbassah, Ibn Touloun's daughter; Beibars' nearby Zaherieh (666).

LXIX. Of the town of Qoft -- Named for Qaftarim; the decline after 400; the
   cupola-marked rich houses; Ibn Ouacif Shah on king Qaftarim (the great miner,
   the bird-trapping pond, his booby-trapped mountain tomb with the 770-year
   inscription); El-Masʿudi on the El-Kharbah emerald mine (the Bedjah guards, the
   lunar brilliance, the body-searches); the year-572 uprising (the false-Daoud
   da'i crushed, ~3,000 hanged from trees).

LXX. Of the town of Dendérah -- Qaftarim's 180-windowed sun-temple (the daily
   light traversing each window); the lion-headed guardian spirit; the "tree of
   Abbas" that wilted and revived at "the axe comes on you"; finer than Akhmim's
   temple.

LXXI. Of the oases of the interior -- The self-sufficient lands in the southwest;
   the Qaftorim/Qaftarim marvels (the standing water-column, the petrifying
   archer-statue, the all-seeing mirror); king Sa's star-warned fifty-cubit-walled
   city (the one Mousa ben Nouseïr found); the sand-buried wonders; the lost-city
   tales (the masons' hidden valley, the travelers' vanishing cities).

LXXII. Of the town of Santariah (Siwa) -- Built by Minaqioush (the first
   riding-grounds and hospital, the "feast of the king"); the white-stone grid-town
   with its seven-tiered circus and sun-turning statue; the present-day tiny
   Santariah (600 Syouah people, the springs, the djinn-haunting).

LXXIII. Of the oases of the exterior -- Built by El-Boudsir; El-Masʿudi's
   geography and the year-332 Laouata ruler; the orange-tree bearing 14,000 fruits;
   the white-alum valley facing Edfou; the Nubian raid (339).

LXXIV. Of the town of Qous -- The largest Said town, built by Sedan ben Adim
   (builder of the Dahchour pyramids and many temples, breeder of the sloughi
   dogs); the "Ghaliath the king" coin (2,300 years old); the deadly scorpions
   ("the pasture of scorpions"); the decline from 800 (17,000 dead in 806, the 150
   abandoned "parks" in the 776 drought).

LXXV. Of the town of Esné -- A year's yield of 40,000 ardebs of dates and 12,000
   of raisins; ~13,000 houses; formerly seventy boulevards.

LXXVI. Of the town of Edfou -- The three-branch palm (each branch a single date);
   the netted enthroned-woman statue with a Greek-inscribed stele, unearthed after
   700.

LXXVII. Of the town of Ahnas -- The claim that Jesus was born there (Mary's
   date-palm surviving to the end of the Umayyads), against the general view of
   Bethlehem; the Bang tree.

LXXVIII. Of the town of El Bahnasa -- The weaving-town of the Bahnasyah hangings
   (each item woven with its recipient's name); the Holy Family's sojourn (the
   Koranic "hill"); the founder Minaoush, first Egyptian ox-worshipper (the
   dream-genie, the speaking bull, the cupola chariots); the buried treasures and
   the djinn-haunted desert town Dîmas; the bull's hollow-gold image and red-stone
   sarcophagus.

LXXIX. Of the town of Ashmouneïn -- One of the Said's greatest towns, built by
   Ashmoun (the mid-Nile glass halls, the under-Nile tunnel to Antinoë, the warning
   copper goose, the serpents magically imprisoned); Orosius making Ashmoun the
   first Egyptian king; the powerful nomad clans (the Béni Ga'far and allies).

LXXX. Of the town of Akhmim -- Built by Minaqioush for the master-magician; the
   great pre-Flood temple (the seven planet-halls, Ibn Djoubeir's 578 measurements,
   Jonah learning alchemy, its demolition in 780); the marvels (the temple-youth,
   the scorpion-attracting wax, the one-legged demon's detachable potency-phallus,
   the 12,000 magicians, the builder Doumaria's model-temple, the eagle-zodiac
   dating to ~30,000 years).

LXXXI. Of the town of El 'Oqab (the Eagle-city) -- Mas'oudi placing it west of
   Abousir; Ibn Ouacif Shah's tale of El-Oualid the Amalecite and his slave 'Oun;
   'Oun's usurpation, the eagle-rescue dream, the gold-eagle worship; the eagle-
   city's construction (the five-colored foundations, the copper gate-eagles, the
   Saturn-consecrated buried pig, the turning eagle-idol); El-Oualid's failed
   reprisal and 'Oun left as governor.

LXXXII. Of the town of El Fayoum -- Joseph reclaiming the Nile-outlet; El-Rian /
   Nahraoush, Pharaoh of Joseph (the delegated vizier Atfeïn, the 365 pleasure-
   pavilions, the Amalecite war, the eleven-year world-campaign to the fog-bound
   seas, the hairy mountain-sage); the full Joseph narrative (the sale and auction,
   Zuleikha's seduction and the finger-cutting banquet, the prison and the dreams,
   the vizierate, the famine, the arrival of Jacob and the priest's prophecy, the
   wandering-then-chained mid-Nile sarcophagus, Sarah revealing the bones to Moses);
   the foundation accounts of the Fayoum (the El-Goubah pit drained in seventy days,
   "elf youm"; the 360 villages; the first nilometer and the engineer's art).

LXXXIII. Of what has been said of the Fayoum, its canals and villages --
   El-Ya'qoubi, El-Mas'oudi, El-Qoda'ï, and Ibn Radouan on the province; the cadi
   El-Makhoumi's year-422 canal register: the "stone of Joseph" wall (a marvel
   rivaling the Pyramids) and the named canals with their Joseph-era gates and
   Coptic-calendar water rules (El-Aouasa, Samastous, Dahalah, Beintawah, Dalah,
   El-Magnounah, Telalah, Samouah, Tabdoud).

LXXXIV. Of the conquest of the Fayoum, its revenues, and its advantages -- The
   year-long search before the guide led the Muslims in (the bloodless fall); the
   variant scout-tales; El-Kandi's praise of the 360-village province; Ibn Zoulaq's
   620,000-dinar yield to Kafour (year 366).

LXXXV. Of the town of El Nahririeh -- The halqah-fief where Sonqor al-Sa'di built
   a mosque (683-685) and shops (later the emir Cheïkhou's waqf); the town's growth
   with silk-weaving; Sonqor's rise to emir, his Sa'idiah madrasah (715) and women's
   ribât, and his death at Tripoli (728). -- END OF THE SECOND PART, AND OF THE WORK.
