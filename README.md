# The Occult Physics (La Physique Occulte), 1693 — Complete English Edition

A full English rendering of **"La Physique Occulte, ou Traité de la Baguette Divinatoire"** by Pierre Le Lorrain de Vallemont (Paris, Jean Anisson, 1693) — the 646-page treatise on the divining rod, written in defense of Jacques Aymar, the Dauphiné peasant who allegedly tracked the Lyon murderers with a hazel fork. Seventeen chapters of corpuscle physics, plague lore, hygrometers, magic lanterns, weapon-salve recipes, a catalogue of 150 French mines, and a closing horoscope cast by the Abbé Gallet of Carpentras.

## Final files

**La_Physique_Occulte_1693_Modern_Illustrated_English.html** (6.9 MB)
The finished edition. The complete book in plain modern English, typeset in a period design (aged paper, rubricated chapter heads, red drop caps, fleuron chapter ends), with all 24 engravings from the original 1693 printing embedded at full resolution. Fully self-contained — one file, no internet needed except for the display fonts, works offline in any browser, prints cleanly. Linked table of chapters at the top.

**La_Physique_Occulte_1693_Modern_English.txt** (~88,800 words)
The same modern text as plain text: title page, dedicatory epistle, preface, table of chapters, all 17 chapters, and the King's Privilege. Engraving positions are marked with bracketed notes.

**LPO_Plates.zip** (4.7 MB)
All 24 engravings extracted from the original at 200 dpi, named and numbered in book order: the title page, the Pollart dedication leaf, the four manners of holding the rod, the transpiration column, three hygrometers, the double barometer, Sturmius's bladder machine, the four-elements vials, the Persian water-pipe, the magic lantern, the three-candles experiment, the seven planetary Vergas, and Jacques Aymar's horoscope.

## Earlier version (kept for reference)

**La_Physique_Occulte_1693_Complete_English.txt** and **La_Physique_Occulte_1693_Illustrated_English.html** — the first pass, a closer scholarly translation in deliberately 17th-century English ("There exhale, by the insensible transpiration..."). Same content, same plates, harder to read. The Modern files above supersede them.

All other .txt files in this folder (LPO*, M*, laphysique*) are working files from the translation and modernization passes — the per-chapter drafts the final versions were built from. They can be ignored or deleted.

## Notes on the rendering

- Content is complete: every name, citation, Latin quotation (with bracketed translations), experiment, recipe, and the full mine catalogue are preserved. Nothing was abridged.
- Structural labels from the original (EXPERIMENT., PHENOMENON., OBJECTION., ANSWER., DIFFICULTY.) are kept.
- The Abbé Gallet's concluding discourse, originally in Latin, is translated in full, including Aymar's natal chart reading.
- Source: the 1693 first printing (registered 6 March 1693, finished printing 21 March 1693).
